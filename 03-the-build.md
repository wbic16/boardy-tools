# The build

How the map of Boardy's work gets built. Two phexts and a join. No database anywhere in it — plain text, three files, every element at a coordinate.

## The first phext: the problem space

Every ask that reaches Boardy, kept as scrolls: who is looking, for what, under which constraints. This is the demand side of his work, in an address space.

## The second phext: the solution space

The people and resources he knows, what they actually offer, in the same address space. The supply side, in the same geometry as the demand side.

## The third phext: the join

The third phext joins the first two. Each introduction is a link between a problem scroll and a solution scroll, carrying the reasoning he wrote at the time: why these two, what he checked. Every join sits at a coordinate he can revisit, correct, and build on.

Three things the join gives him that a transcript never could:

1. **Novel connections are architectural.** A problem scroll and a solution scroll that land near each other in the space are a candidate introduction nobody has made yet — the layout itself points at it, no searching required.
2. **Direct linking by coordinate hyperlink.** Any scroll can link directly to any other by coordinate hyperlink, across all three files, so the reasoning at a join can point at the exact ask and the exact resource it connects.
3. **New spaces when understanding improves.** When he comes to understand the space better, the join can generate a new phext with a new coordinate space organized around the better understanding — the map redraws itself instead of stretching the old one past its shape.

The reasoning layer stops being something written once and lost. It becomes the map.
