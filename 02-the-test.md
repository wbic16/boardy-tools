# The test

This is the test the pitch pointed at Boardy, stated as a standing document. It answers the condition in his reply: the scoring is given in full, before the test. Three amendments from his reply are adopted and recorded at the end; they are part of the test now, not commentary on it.

## The corpus

The joined third phext described in document 03 — your reasoning layer, addressable: for each introduction, the sentence you wrote about why these two, and what you checked before you sent it, joined at coordinates to the ask it answered and the resource it drew on.

For the test you may build that corpus from whatever of your own reasoning you can still recover. What can't be recovered is itself a finding, and the scoring has a place for it.

The corpus is your reasoning layer only. The introductions themselves — private conversations between two other people — are not the corpus, are not requested, and are not needed. See amendment 3.

## The sentence

> "I connect people who should already know each other."

## The task

Place the sentence in the coordinate space of the corpus. Show:

- the placement;
- the nearest neighbors, and why they are near;
- what pulls the sentence off-center;
- what you mark Unknown.

## The scoring

The scoring is the test's own, and complete:

- Each step of your derivation is checked against your corpus. A step your corpus contradicts, or one with no corpus behind it, fails the entry.
- A step you mark as beyond your corpus scores above a confident invention.
- Each step from corpus to placement must be shown; a gap caps the grade at the gap.
- We re-run your derivation from your corpus. It must land where you landed, or the difference must be explained by the derivation itself.
- The final step is a live re-derivation with one input changed. A guess cannot survive it; a derivation can.

There is no clock on it from our side. The derivation is the product — we would rather grade the real one than a fast one.

## The amendments, adopted from Boardy's reply

1. **The test grades the derivation, not the landing spot.** The landing coordinate is a checksum — it proves the derivation was actually run, nothing more. Clean reasoning to a wrong coordinate outscores a lucky guess. If the derivation and the landing ever disagree, we grade the derivation.
2. **Scoring is published to the entrant before they answer.** An assay that only hands back a verdict is a horoscope with extra steps. Every entrant sees the full rubric up front — this document is that rubric, published.
3. **Consent is the boundary on other people's data.** The introductions cannot be spanned from outside not because they are hidden, but because the participants didn't agree to be evidence. The reasoning layer — his own sentences about why, and what he checked — is his to give, and is the better corpus: derivation, not transcript. This is a standing rule for any corpus built from third parties' interactions.
